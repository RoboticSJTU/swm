**Evidence rules:**

Instruction defines task requirements. Reference clarifies intended results and necessary dependencies. The image establishes the current visible initial state and clarifies identities; it does not add task requirements.

Candidate `Before` and `State change` are claims to verify, not established facts. `+` adds a fact; `-` removes one.

# Audit

## 1. Define and ground requirements

- Identify required objects, quantities, operations, methods, relations, and final conditions. Required actions must be performed, even if the final state already holds (e.g., pick up and return the cup). Verify all requirements; collective symbols must represent actual items and executable transfers, not just quantities implied by their names.
- Ground each object, source, and target in the actual scene. Accept different labels only when they uniquely identify the same physical entity or region: for example, `counter`/`table`, `cup`/`mug`, or `top_drawer`/`cabinet` when both clearly denote the same pickup surface. Do not assume equivalence among competing referents or substitute an indirect support, containment, or parent-child relation for the actual source or target (e.g., a kettle rests on its base, and the base rests on the table; stating that the kettle is directly on the table is incorrect).
- Default to no ordering requirement unless Instruction explicitly specifies one (e.g., `before`, `after`, `then`) or an evidence-supported execution dependency requires it. Commas, `and`, and Reference order alone do not impose a sequence.

## 2. Establish initial facts and dependencies

- Start from the actual scene, not an assumed earlier workflow stage. Use clear image evidence for initial facts; when unclear or incomplete, rely on Reference for initial locations, holding, and states. Nonvisibility does not imply absence. Preserve established holding and hand occupancy; another pickup requires a prior release. Reference starting with placement implies initial holding, but later effects are not initial facts.
- Preserve compatible facts: support and holding may coexist; a broom may rest on the floor while leaning against a wall or cabinet. Support does not imply orientation, nor visibility imply access. Unsupported facts remain unknown.
- Classify Reference actions as required operations, enabling steps, restoration, termination, or redundant demonstration. Enforce necessary access, clearance, support, resource availability, and device states even if Candidate omits them. Preparation may be skipped only if already satisfied or equivalently established. Incidental staging may differ; sequence alone does not establish dependency.

## 3. Verify execution and completion

**Candidate trace:**
{evaluated_symbolic_trace}

**Verification:**

- Follow only Candidate. Interpret action names, arguments, prerequisites, and effects together. Verify actual source/target, holding, occupancy, access, support, device/container states, and interlocks before accepting effects. Occupied hands must release or transfer loads before incompatible acquisitions; simultaneous resources must be distinct. Extra actions must be executable, and declared effects cannot replace physical transitions.
- Track current states and completed operations separately; preserve valid facts and remove superseded ones. Contact, tool engagement, and local reorientation may be implicit, but transfers, relocation, obstacle removal, and resource release may not. Verify receiving positions at each operating site (e.g., outlets), without treating unspecified fine positioning as evidence of a wrong site. Poured mixtures retain their ingredients.
- Water supply requires explicit turn-on and turn-off operations; filling containers or transferring water symbols replaces neither. Other active processes persist until explicitly stopped or supported automatic completion.
- Check prerequisites at execution time, stage conditions when required, and final goals after all actions. Later actions cannot repair earlier infeasibility but may alter temporary states. Verify all required operations, quantities, dependencies, final relations, restoration, and termination.

**Decision:**

- Fail only for an evidence-supported execution conflict or unmet requirement. Identify the first invalid action or missing requirement, citing the Instruction, initial image, or Reference.
- Do not invent hidden states, intentions, relations, parameter roles, or intermediate handling. Uncertainty, unexplained flags, naming differences, ambiguous support, 2D proximity/overlap, speculative physical constraints, hand preference, or independent ordering alone do not justify failure.
- Pass only if the executable Candidate satisfies all requirements and necessary dependencies.
