## Representation

- Use untyped PDDL: static unary categories in init and action guards, never :types or typed declarations. Declare :strips, adding :negative-preconditions and :equality only when used. Conditions/goals are conjunctions of atoms or atomic negations; effects only add/delete atoms. No quantifiers, disjunctions, conditional effects, derived predicates, numeric fluents, or durative actions.
- Use the simplest causally sufficient representation. Introduce structural or control relations only when needed to bind action participants correctly or express necessary dependencies. When a component's separate source or target role matters, bind it to its whole with part_of and put direct support/containment relations on the component.
- Model only task-relevant entities and distinctions. Merge fixed doors, handles, and controls into their functional whole unless independent identity, state, location, or operation matters. Keep detachable parts and independently operated compartments distinct.
- Put access state on the container/compartment, switch state on the device, pose on the moved object, and process results on the affected material/object. For a single-door appliance, open/closed belongs to the appliance. Track separate doors only when their independent opening matters; do not duplicate their access state on the whole.
- Use the predicate vocabulary below for matching meanings; extend it only for necessary new semantics, with fixed argument roles and a clear lifecycle. Omit redundant capabilities, scene details, and synonymous facts.

## Transitions

- One operator represents one completed manipulation or waiting event with its necessary participants, prerequisites, resource changes, and direct results. Keep independently completed acquisition, use, handoff, placement, and start/stop events separate; omit incidental contact and internal motion.
- Pick acquires holding and removes direct support/containment; place releases holding and establishes the target relation. Transfer finite contents by deleting the source relation and adding the destination; unlimited supplies use dispenses. Preserve unrelated pose, payload, and structural relations. Commit process results only at their supported completion boundary, never merely at startup or an arbitrary shutdown.
- clear means no modeled object is directly on that object. Preserve the moved object's clear during pick/place unless its own loads change. Adding on invalidates the support's clear; establish clear only after its last direct load is removed. Changes to in alone do not change clear. Track clear only for relevant objects, but keep a shared operator's causal support updates consistent across its sources. Do not require an entire multi-object surface to be clear merely to place another object there.
- If a nested container or cover blocks an opening independently of open/closed, use uncovered for that necessary aperture constraint. Ordinary contents do not automatically obstruct the opening. Update it on actual covering/uncovering and restore it only after the last blocker is removed. Prevent placement into a carried object's own descendant; preserve legitimate carried contents instead of forbidding their acquisition to avoid containment cycles.
- For autonomous device processes where the robot waits for a result, use wait_until_<OBJECT>_<RESULT>[_<RELATION>_<ENTITY>]*. Require the necessary causal conditions and establish only the supported completion result. Keep independent start/stop actions separate and preserve ongoing hand occupancy.
- Each hand has unit capacity. Account for acquisition, release, sustained control, and distinct simultaneous hands without unsupported hand preferences. Require accessible targets and acyclic support/containment; use (not (= ?x ?y)) only for roles that must be distinct.
- Keep only poses required by a later action or the goal. Omit transient tilt/reset inside pouring or other operations. A necessary pose change requires the evidenced old pose and replaces it with the new one; an unchanged pose is preserved.
- Encode required operations and ordering through real causal dependencies. If final physical state cannot prove a required event, use a reusable event relation bound to its essential participants; no step counters, phases, or generic completion flags. Do not force independent actions into demonstration order.

## Naming and layout

OPERATION_OBJECT[_RESULT][_<RELATION>_<ENTITY>]*

- Use canonical operation words and functional categories, with relation roles ordered source, target, tool. For the same family, use the same fields and prepositions: pick_*_from_*, place_*_on/in_*, pour_*_from_*_into_*, scoop_*_from_*_with_*, insert_*_into_*, remove_*_from_*, open_*, close_*, turn_on/off_*, wipe_*_with_*. Extend to new operations by the same rule.
- Include indispensable nested support/containment relations in names. Use in/on for positive relations and outside for required exclusion from a container; the name must match the condition's polarity. Remove noncausal context instead of naming it.
- RESULT names only a necessary core result not already expressed by the operation. Include pose only when this action establishes that necessary result; a pose prerequisite alone does not enter its name. Use one name and model when a common causal contract applies across sources. When necessary differences in operation mode, source/target relations, or physical constraints require different models, distinguish them with concise names.
- Use lowercase snake_case. Instances, appearance, numbering suffixes, and incidental context stay out of action names. Tools are separate handheld auxiliaries, not hands or built-in controls. Use *_top in names only to distinguish a container's exterior support from its interior.
- Order parameters by hand(s), core object, source, target, tool, then other necessary participants. List category guards first. Keep each precondition/effect on one line, with each old-state deletion adjacent to its new-state addition.
