Infer events from changes in the physical scene. Instructions and earlier proposals guide attention, not occurrence. First locate the SAME object independently at both endpoints: match its visible boundaries, shape/color and relation to surrounding objects. Keep one short stable name per instance. An overlapping hand or object does not make two objects one; identify which body moves and which stays supported. Occluded means unknown, not absent, lifted or released.

Track control, support/containment, location, orientation, contents and device conditions, using object-to-hand/surface relations rather than image coordinates. For attached controls, compare angle/opening relative to the fixed housing: operation need not translate the object. A hand coupled to a control can reveal its turn/press even when the contact obscures it; the internal mechanism need not be visible. Unrelated hand/camera motion or contact alone is not an effect. Include supported preparation and ending operations even for objects absent from the instruction.

An event is a supported manipulation result or use. Pick leaves a support; Grasp establishes task control without requiring a lift; Place ends control on a support. Move expresses observed relocation when finer phases or the destination are unclear. Preserve distinct acquisition, use, handoff and placement effects. Group boundaries do not create events: merge progress of one continuous operation. A release/reacquisition or reversal requires evidence for BOTH transitions, not two differently worded proposals. Omit reaching, static holding and incidental trajectory subdivisions.

Follow contents with their carrier. Loading followed by opening-down tipping over a compatible target and subsequent return supports unloading even when small contents are occluded. Inspect the ending object-to-support and hand relations: a final grasp, lift or relocation can be an event without a later release. Do not assume that continued holding means no change.

Before/After are brief evidenced facts about control, direct support/location, contents or device conditions. Before precedes the whole event; After gives its result. Omit execution poses and guessed settings/functionality; use an empty string for an unknown state. Keep control/support continuous between events unless a change is evidenced.
