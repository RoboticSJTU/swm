1. Observe physical relations
- Follow the same object using its shape, boundaries and stable surroundings; keep one name per instance at the supported detail level.
- Compare control, support/containment, orientation, contents and device state relative to hands and fixed surfaces; distinguish object motion from hand/camera motion.
- Establish facts from images; use the instruction and action history to guide attention.
- Image overlap does not establish contact; contact alone does not establish control, pressing or placement.
- Occlusion means unknown, not absent, lifted, contained or released; leave hidden intermediate events unspecified.

2. Identify independent events
- Preserve distinct supported acquisition, use, handoff and placement results, including relevant operations on objects outside the instruction.
- Pick establishes control and lifts clear of support; Grasp establishes control, possibly while supported; Place transfers support to the destination as hand control ends.
- An acquisition needs its own visible control/support transition. Initial holding or later Pour/Place alone does not establish an earlier Pick.
- Merge grasp/release phases into the corresponding pickup, removal, handoff or placement; retain standalone Grasp when establishing control is itself an independent event.
- Choose the specificity supported by the evidence. Clear observed endpoints can establish an action; actual relocation can be described as Move when finer phases remain uncertain.

3. Interpret use and controls
- Judge attached controls by angle/opening relative to the housing or coupled hand-control motion, including partially occluded operation. Name the function supported by visible control evidence or device response.
- Follow contents with their carrier. For Pour/Unload, use visible flow or established contents/loading followed by opening-down tipping of an open carrier over a receiving target. Subsequent carrier motion or return can help confirm transfer through occlusion.

4. Follow continuous trajectories
- Recognize an occurrence once, when its defining evidence first becomes sufficient. Ongoing use can be recognized as soon as it is clearly visible.
- Record preparation and continuation as observations across groups. Distinguish occurrences by physical transitions rather than repeated names or group boundaries.
- Give each repeat or reversal independent evidence: repeated acquisition requires ended and renewed control; a reversal requires both opposing state transitions.
- Include supported terminal acquisitions, lifts or relocations, even when a later release lies outside the video.

Boundary examples
- Overlap: A hand passes above a block, but the block stays on its support and no coupled control is visible. Record the observation without Grasp or Pick.
- Already held: A group starts with a bottle already held and then shows liquid pouring into a cup. Recognize Pour, but infer no Pick from this group alone; the Compiler may recover an acquisition only from earlier image evidence.
- Ending lift: A tray changes from table-supported to held clear of the table at the video end. Recognize the pickup/lift even without a later release; do not label it Place.
